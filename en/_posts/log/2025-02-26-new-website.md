It's been a while now since I wanted to completely rebuild my personal website. I wrote about it in the previous post, more than a year ago. 

While keeping its concept of "one thread to rule them all", I abandoned the personal timeline mentioned below and started fresh on this beginning of the year. 

I write, I make performances, installations, I code sites and games, I do kind of a wide range of activities, paid or unpaid, and I wanted a place gathering all of them. I wanted to stop categorizing between domains, mediums, and most of all between "professional activities" (aka paid) and "amateur activities" (aka for free). I also wanted to stop considering something important or valid just because it had been judged so by a third party institution or by some public display. Some projects aren't photogenic, others never even take a concrete shape, some of them will never stop being WIPs, but they still exist. I wanted to put researches and fully-achieved projects on the same level. I wanted to unburden myself with the urge for results and to be fully able to thrive on the process.

I also wanted a place for myself away from social media. Somewhere I could write down my thoughts instead of publishing a story on Instagram, somewhere I could tell my friends to go to if they wanted some news, instead of randomly appearing in someone else's feed without having been summoned there.

Well, I wanted a blog. I wanted some personal space on the internet. Somewhere I could do what I want, when I want, under the format that I want.

I pondered a lot wether it would be a better idea to build it from scratch or to rely on a good old SSG. Time was the key. I needed this site as quickly as possible, cause I wanted to write in it as soon as possible. I quit my job in december and I wanted this brand new place to be ready for my new beginning. I then just built up a classic static site with Jekyll, which is the SSG I know the most. I wrote down a SCSS file greatly inspired by the one existing here. I added a tiny-tiny-tiny bit of JS, to override Jekyll paginator.

[And I'm really happy with all that!](https://luciedesaubliaux.fr)[^1]

[^1]: Sorry, it's in french only. While I know it would be great to translate it in english in order to integrate the great community of bloggers and personal website crafters I've observed with great enthusiasm for years, it would still feel very presomptuous of me to pretend I've got a lot of international readers. Of course, if you'd like to advise me otherwise, I would be greatly flattered to hear from you.
