While I already knew I'd completely enjoy the process of building its database, its forms and its display pages, I had never created a research system to browse through all the data and was dubious about whether I could do it on my own.

I postponed this part of the project to the very end because it was really frightening me...

... and it was, in fact, a really fun thing to do.

First, I had to ponder which data were relevant to browse through. What were the interesting things to look for into my database. I had to to wonder what other humans could be interested into while exploring my website. I had to take enough insight to be able to look at my site as if I didn't know it at all. If I were new here, what kind of bits of info would I be looking for? How would I like to be guided? What keywords could I type in order to retrieve something I don't even know is there? How accurate would I be in my search? What is the good balance between complete freedom in my search or precisely-labelled input fields to keep me on tracks?

Then came the translation of all these possible human requests into browser language. Now that I knew what I wanted to retrieve from my database, I had to ask the machine to do it for me. I had to precise which request should be treated exactly how it was anounced, which request was more approximative and how to treat approximation. Human requests are made with words but the machine doesn't understand the meaning of words. The best things the machine can do is to compare words to one another and to order them in mathematical or alphabetical order. Words are like abstract images to a machine and I had to explain it the boundaries of each of those images, their beginning and their end, and whether they were strict or smooth. I had to translate all those words in other words, the words composing PHP or MySQL languages.

To conclude, a research system is where all the links threaded between your database tables, rows and columns are tested. This is how you see if all your drawers are well-labelled, and if, by finding one tiny bit of information, you'll be able to retrieve all the other data that could be related to it or interesting to display alongside it. 

And I just want to build others now.
